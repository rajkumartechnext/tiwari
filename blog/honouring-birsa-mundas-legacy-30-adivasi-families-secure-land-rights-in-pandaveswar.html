Honouring Birsa Munda’s Legacy: 30+ Adivasi Families Secure Land Rights in Pandaveswar

For a family that has farmed the same plot for generations, a piece of paper can feel like a small thing until the day it is missing. On 15 November, the birth anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda, more than 30 Adivasi brothers and sisters received that paper at the Durgapur-Faridpur BDO office in the Pandaveswar constituency. It was a land patta, a legal proof of ownership, handed over to Janjatiya Gaurav Divas.

Why Does Patta Matters More Than It Looks?

A patta is a land ownership document. Without one, a family may live and farm on a plot for decades and still struggle to prove it belongs to them. That uncertainty touches everything: getting a bank loan, claiming a government scheme, passing the land to children, or defending it in a dispute.

With a patta, the picture changes. Ownership becomes official, and that brings security. It also brings something harder to measure, which is self-respect. As the MLA put it in his message, land rights support dignity, safety and a more secure future for the next generation. That is why tribal land rights are never just a paperwork issue. They decide whether a family can plan ahead.

The Man Behind the Day

Birsa Munda was born on 15 November 1875 into the Munda tribe of Chhotanagpur. Tribal communities across the country revere him as Bhagwan. He united the Munda tribes against British land laws and feudal exploitation, and his Ulgulan, the Great Tumult, is still remembered as a movement for social justice.

His struggle was about land from the start. His movement led to protective reforms such as the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act of 1908, which secured tribal land rights, and Jharkhand was created on 15 November 2000, his birth anniversary, as a tribute to his legacy. Handing over pattas on his birthday is a fitting way to honour that history.

What Janjatiya Gaurav Divas Stand For?

The Union Cabinet declared 15 November as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas in 2021, to honour tribal freedom fighters and recognise tribal communities’ contribution to India’s heritage. This year marks his 151st birth anniversary.

The occasion was marked with land distribution elsewhere in the state too. West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari handed land patta deeds to 770 tribal members in Jhargram on the same day.

A Patta Is a Start, Not the Finish Line

It would be too easy to treat a distribution ceremony as the end of the story. Independent research on the subject paints a more careful picture. A 2021 study of tribal land rights in West Bengal noted that many forest patta claims remain pending or rejected, and that tribal land alienation continues to be a concern.

That is exactly why each document handed over carries weight. Families still need correct land records, access to farming support and a place to turn when disputes arise. Tribal land rights become real when the title, the record and the field all match. The follow-through matters as much as the function.

Why This Moment Matters for Pandaveswar?

For the 30-plus families in Durgapur-Faridpur, the patta is proof that their claim to the land has been formally recognised. It gives them something solid to build on, whether that means a better harvest, a bank account backed by real assets, or simply the peace of mind that comes from knowing the ground under their feet is theirs.

For the wider community, it is a reminder that tribute to Birsa Munda is best paid through action. Speeches fade by evening. A land title lasts for generations.
